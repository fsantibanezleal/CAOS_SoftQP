## Dynamics and independent checks

For the double integrator, the future state is the zero-force offset plus the causal response to each held force. At future step k and control j, the position response is the one-step force coefficient plus (k-j) time steps of the velocity coefficient. The velocity response stays constant for all k at or after j. The prediction cost expands into the stored positive definite P and frame-specific q, with the constant offset term removed for all methods. Physical bounds subtract the appropriate zero-force forecast offset. The software tests check all 32 frames against explicit recursive simulation for both the forecast and the reference input, including the impulses and the post-impulse held actions.

The two-zone thermal source uses one initialization row and 16 hard forecast recurrences per zone. The 32 heat rows have exact [0,6] bounds. The 32 soft rows select only future temperatures and retain degree-Celsius comfort units. Zero heat and its explicitly rolled temperatures form a hard-feasible anchor. The N13 peak groups are disjoint by zone. N14 stores original lower/upper comfort prices and uses the exact nominal symmetric lift when their ratio is one.

The thermal equation pattern comes from the authored [MIT-licensed product source](https://github.com/fsantibanezleal/CAOS_SoftQP/blob/bbdd7909b2ee523881383764eb969eaab079d4de/data-pipeline/pipeline/coupled_cases.py) at blob `025f0c7b25bd721961c6fa79add34d873db6e307`. The generator produces independent R4 coefficients, forecasts and layout under the source specification.

The source and contract checks use the pinned product Python environment and its existing NumPy/SciPy. Fixture elapsed time and file sizes are engineering observations. The 8-GiB data/checkpoint and RSS limits, 600-second scientific preflight, external output admission and no-redraw policy remain gates for registered execution.
