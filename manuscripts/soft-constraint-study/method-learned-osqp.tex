\subsection{Learned primal-dual initialization for OSQP}\label{sec:method-learned-osqp}
The fixed-point warm-start approach of Sambharya et al.
(\href{https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume25/23-1174/23-1174.pdf}{primary source})
learns an initial iterate for an optimization algorithm. In SoftQP the
initial iterate is for stock OSQP \cite{osqp} on the admitted convex lift.
The learned map leaves the objective, constraints and stopping tests
of the final solve fixed. The model applies only to a supported
dimension head and a corresponding admitted model and normalizer.

Let \(P,A,q,l,u\) be the dimensionless lifted Hessian, interval matrix,
linear cost and bounds, with \(n\) variables and \(m\) rows. Features in
order are the upper triangle of \(P\), row-major \(A\), \(q\), lower
values, upper values, and the two finite-side masks. An infinite
endpoint is encoded by a zero value and a false mask. The feature
dimension is \(n(n+1)/2+nm+n+4m\). The CPU binary64 network has
two rectified 128-wide hidden layers and \(n+m\) output coordinates,
interpreted as a primal and a signed interval-dual initialization.
Each head uses its own train-only coordinate normalizer and ordered
input dimensions.

For the differentiated training iterate, let \(w\) be the interval
splitting state and \(S=P+\sigma I+\rho A^\top A\).
The SDK initializes \(w^0=Ax^0\) from the model output and uses
\begin{align}
 S x^{k+1}&=\sigma x^k-q+A^\top(\rho w^k-y^k),\label{eq:learn-osqp-x}\\
 w^{k+1}&=\Pi_{[l,u]}(Ax^{k+1}+y^k/\rho),\label{eq:learn-osqp-w}\\
 y^{k+1}&=y^k+\rho(Ax^{k+1}-w^{k+1}).\label{eq:learn-osqp-y}
\end{align}
Here \(\rho=0.1\) and \(\sigma=10^{-6}\). A positive \(\sigma\) makes
\(S\) positive definite for positive semidefinite \(P\) in exact
arithmetic; an invalid or nonrepresentable matrix is rejected.
The implementation factors \(S\) once per constructed training
problem and uses zero derivative at each projection kink, including
coincident endpoints. After five transitions the sixth supplies
the residual for the training loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:learn-osqp-loss}
 \mathcal L=\tfrac13\left(
 \frac{\lVert x^6-x^5\rVert_2^2}{n}
 +\frac{\lVert y^6-y^5\rVert_2^2}{m}
 +\frac{\lVert w^6-w^5\rVert_2^2}{m}\right).
\end{equation}
This objective uses no target solution and does not certify the
optimization result. The network learns an initializer rather than
a new update operator.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Train and apply a dimension-specific OSQP initializer}\label{alg:learn-osqp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Fit each head and its normalizer using the eligible training sources.
\State Select the checkpoint by source-weighted validation loss.
\State Validate the selected head and normalizer for the input dimension.
\State Predict and rescale the initial primal and signed-dual vectors.
\State Pass them to stock OSQP, and independently check its recovered result.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The fit specifies fourteen heads for each of seeds 41001--41003,
source-balanced loss and AdamW with 32 sources per batch,
learning rate \(10^{-3}\), decay \(10^{-4}\), at most 100 epochs,
patience 20 and minimum improvement \(10^{-6}\). A trajectory shares
its source weight across frames. At inference the lift maps
dimensionless predictions to backend coordinates using decision
scales \(d_x\) and signed-dual scales \(s_f/d_r\), with \(s_f\)
the objective scale and \(d_r\) the row scales. Stock OSQP 1.1.3
receives the physical lift and the resulting vectors in
\texttt{warm\_start}. The matched cold and previous controls use the
same solver settings, with zero vectors or the last accepted
physical lifted vectors on the same contiguous trajectory.
They use no numerical factor or solver workspace from the prior
frame. The matched settings are \(10^{-7}\) absolute and relative
tolerances, scalar \(\rho=0.1\), \(\sigma=10^{-6}\), \(\alpha=1\),
\(200\,000\) iterations, per-iteration termination checking, no
rescaling, adaptive penalty or polishing. Predictor cost includes
feature assembly and dense network products. Native cost also
includes setup, sparse solve, and recovery and checks. Missing or
nonfinite predictions, unsupported dimensions, or an unaccepted
native result leave that attempt unavailable or failed. Calibration
does not apply to this method.
